## Description
In 1916, Germany's top general planned a battle that wasn't about winning ground. It was meant to kill Frenchmen faster than France could replace them. It nearly destroyed Germany's army too.

For ten months in 1916, French and German soldiers fought over a few miles of hills around the fortress city of Verdun. Erich von Falkenhayn's plan, Operation Gericht, relied on massed artillery and a target France could not abandon. This video follows how the trap was set, how France held on, and what the battle cost both nations.

In this video we cover:
• Falkenhayn's logic, and the disputed "Christmas memorandum"
• Operation Gericht: the 21 February bombardment, Driant at the Bois des Caures, and the fall of Fort Douaumont
• Pétain, the Voie Sacrée and the noria rotation of French divisions
• Mort-Homme, Hill 304 and the siege of Fort Vaux, including the pigeon Le Vaillant
• Green Cross gas, Fleury, and Nivelle's "They shall not pass"
• The Somme and Brusilov, Falkenhayn's fall, and the French recapture of Douaumont
• The cost: about 714,000 casualties, the destroyed villages and the Douaumont Ossuary
