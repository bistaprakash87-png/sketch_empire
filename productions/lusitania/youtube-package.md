## Description
In 1915, one German torpedo sank the Lusitania in eighteen minutes and killed nearly 1,200 people, 128 of them American. So why did the United States wait two more years to go to war?

The Lusitania was one of the fastest liners in the world, sailing through a war zone Germany had declared around Britain. A German embassy warning ran in New York newspapers on the morning she left. Six days later, the U-boat U-20 caught her off the coast of Ireland. What followed was outrage, propaganda and a diplomatic tightrope, and it all ended with America at war in April 1917.

In this video we cover:
• America's neutrality and Germany's 1915 war zone around Britain
• The German embassy's warning printed beside the Lusitania's sailing advert
• The 18 minutes off the Old Head of Kinsale, and the mystery second explosion
• American fury, Roosevelt's anger, and the debate over the ship's cargo
• Wilson's "too proud to fight" speech, the Lusitania notes and Bryan's resignation
• The Goetz medal and Britain's propaganda war
• The Arabic and Sussex pledges, and "He kept us out of war"
• Unrestricted submarine warfare, the Zimmermann Telegram and the 1917 vote for war
