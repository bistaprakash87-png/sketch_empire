# Script: LEGO — from a Danish carpenter's workshop to the world's favourite toy

16:9 · 97 lines · 1358 words · est. 9:00

## Hook

1. In 2025, LEGO made more than eighty billion Danish kroner selling little plastic bricks.
2. It all began in a small Danish town, with a carpenter who had run out of customers.
3. By 2015, his little company had overtaken Mattel as the world's biggest toy maker.
4. But in 2004, the same company lost almost two billion kroner and was fighting to survive.
5. So how does a small carpenter's workshop become a brand the whole world loves?
6. Along the way came three fires, an economic crisis and a near-collapse.
7. This is the story of LEGO, and the crisis that nearly ended it.

## A Carpenter in Billund

8. Billund is a small town on the flat farmland of Jutland, in Denmark.
9. Ole Kirk Christiansen was born in 1891, the tenth child of a poor Jutland family.
10. He was apprenticed as a carpenter at fourteen, then worked in Germany for five years.
11. Back in Denmark, he bought a carpentry shop and lumberyard in Billund.
12. He married Kirstine Sørensen in 1916, and together they started a family.
13. His wife Kirstine died after the birth of their fourth child, leaving him to raise the family.
14. Then in 1924, his young sons accidentally set wood shavings alight, and the workshop and home burned down.
15. He rebuilt, and his workshop sold furniture and ladders, until the Great Depression arrived.
16. That crisis would push him toward a much smaller product and a much bigger idea.

## Play Well

17. By early 1932, he had laid off all but seven workers.
18. Few people could afford furniture, so he turned to small, cheap wooden goods.
19. Of everything he made, the toys sold best, so he decided to focus on them.
20. His first toy line, launched in 1932, included wooden cars, aeroplanes and yo-yos.
21. He needed a name for his new toys, so he held a contest among his workers.
22. The winning name joined two Danish words, leg godt, which mean play well.
23. By 1939, he had finished repaying a loan of three thousand crowns, which his siblings had guaranteed.
24. On the night of 20 March 1942, fire struck again and destroyed his factory.
25. Offers came to rebuild elsewhere in Denmark, but he chose to stay in Billund.
26. A year after the fire, he already employed forty people again.
27. He rebuilt with a bank loan, and the factory reopened in 1944 with an assembly line.

## The Brick

28. After the Second World War, a strange new material reached Denmark: plastic.
29. LEGO bought its first plastic injection moulding machine in 1947, paying thirty thousand crowns.
30. A British firm, Kiddicraft, had already designed interlocking plastic bricks, and LEGO studied its samples.
31. By 1949, LEGO was selling its own version, called Automatic Binding Bricks.
32. The bricks were renamed LEGO Mursten in 1953, which simply means LEGO Bricks.
33. Licensed production began in Norway that year, and in 1955 in Sweden and Iceland.
34. At first the bricks sold poorly, and retailers sent many shipments back.
35. Earlier bricks were hollow underneath, so models wobbled and fell apart.
36. From 1950, Godtfred, Ole Kirk's son, spent about a decade improving the idea.
37. On 28 January 1958, at 1:58 in the afternoon, LEGO filed its patent for a toy building element.
38. Studs on one brick press into tubes on another, creating a grip called clutch power.
39. Six ordinary two-by-four bricks can be joined in over nine hundred million different ways.
40. That year, the LEGO system of play included twenty-eight sets and eight vehicles.
41. Ole Kirk Christiansen died in 1958, and Godtfred took over the company.
42. A warehouse fire in 1960 destroyed most of the company's wooden toy stock.
43. Godtfred then ended wooden toy production and bet the company on plastic bricks.
44. By the end of 1960, LEGO employed more than five hundred people.

## A Toy for the World

45. In 1961, LEGO let Samsonite make and sell its bricks in the United States and Canada.
46. Around 1962, the first LEGO wheels let builders make cars that actually rolled.
47. A year later, LEGO swapped cellulose acetate for ABS plastic, the material its bricks are made from today.
48. Instruction manuals first appeared in the boxes in 1964, so builders could follow a plan.
49. A train system with a small battery motor followed in 1966.
50. Legoland opened in Billund in 1968 and welcomed 625,000 visitors in its first year.
51. Duplo arrived in 1969, with bricks twice as big for younger children.
52. The first North American LEGO factory opened in Enfield, Connecticut, in 1973.
53. August 1978 brought the minifigure, a tiny person that gave every model a hero.
54. Godtfred's son Kjeld Kirk Kristiansen became president in 1979, the third generation to lead the company.
55. An education department followed in 1980, adapting bricks for classrooms.
56. In 1985, LEGO opened a factory in Manaus, Brazil, giving the brick a South American home.
57. LEGO Pirates, launched in 1989, showed that sets could tell stories, not just build things.
58. By 1990, LEGO ranked among the world's ten biggest toy companies, the only European one.

## Too Many Ideas

59. Then the company lost its focus, one new idea at a time.
60. After a sales slump in 1993, LEGO tripled the number of products it offered.
61. It moved into theme parks, video games, jewellery, clothing and watches.
62. Some experienced designers were replaced with recent graduates, and profits had slipped since 1992.
63. In 1998, LEGO posted its first loss in its history and laid off a thousand workers.
64. The first licensed sets arrived in 1999, including Star Wars and Winnie the Pooh Duplo.
65. Licensing helped, yet the company kept growing more complicated and more expensive to run.

## The Brink

66. In 2003, LEGO ran a deficit of 1.4 billion Danish kroner, about 220 million dollars.
67. Poul Plougmann lost the top job, and the founder's grandson Kjeld Kirk Kristiansen stepped back in.
68. The next year, the net loss grew to almost 1.9 billion kroner on sales of 7.9 billion.
69. That meant the company lost nearly a quarter of everything it sold.
70. Reports say its debt roughly equalled a year of sales, and buyers were waiting.
71. Kjeld Kirk Kristiansen then put 800 million kroner of the family's own money into the company.
72. In Billund, a company founded in a wooden workshop now faced its gravest year.
73. Jørgen Vig Knudstorp, a former McKinsey consultant, became chief executive in 2004.
74. He concluded that LEGO had drifted from its core, the plastic brick.
75. Everything that was not the brick, he decided, had to go.

## Back to the Brick

76. He sold properties in Australia, the United States and South Korea, plus the video game unit.
77. LEGO sold control of its Legoland parks to Merlin Entertainments in 2005.
78. He cut the number of different pieces by about half, and outsourced much of the production.
79. Between 1998 and 2007, LEGO's global workforce shrank from about 9,100 to 4,200.
80. Factories closed too, including the plant in Enfield, Connecticut, in 2006.
81. By 2006, just two years after the record loss, LEGO was back in profit.
82. He also listened to adult fans, and some fan designs submitted through LEGO Ideas reached shop shelves.
83. Licensed themes like Star Wars, Marvel and Harry Potter brought in new audiences.
84. Global sales rose 18 percent in 2008, and in the United States they jumped 32 percent.
85. LEGO also invented its own hit theme, Ninjago, which launched in 2011.
86. By 2013, LEGO was reported as the world's most profitable toymaker.
87. The LEGO Movie arrived in 2014 with one of the highest openings recorded for an original animated film.
88. In the first half of 2015, LEGO sold 2.1 billion dollars of toys, passing Mattel's 1.9 billion.
89. Revenue fell in 2017 for the first time in thirteen years, and LEGO announced job cuts.
90. By 2025, revenue reached 83.5 billion kroner, up twelve percent in a single year.
91. Consumer sales grew sixteen percent that year, more than double the toy market's seven percent.
92. The company that nearly collapsed now employs about thirty-one thousand people.
93. In 2026, LEGO bought 29 LEGO and Legoland Discovery Centres from Merlin, the company that took its parks.

## Conclusion

94. Three fires, an economic crisis and a near-collapse could not end it.
95. Its rescue always came back to one simple idea: pieces that click together.
96. And the name Ole Kirk chose, play well, turned out to be the whole strategy.
97. A struggling carpenter's workshop became a brand the whole world loves, one brick at a time.
